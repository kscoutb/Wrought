THE QUESTION. _reap_refusals() refuses to signal a process in the runner's own SESSION.
If a real gate's guest were in that session, the guard would silently disable the sweep —
a safety fix that breaks the safety control it protects.

THE ANSWER, from the code and from the proof run:

## cmd: grep -n 'start_new_session' bin/wrought-runner
748:                                cwd=cfg["gate_cwd"], start_new_session=True)

The gate child is launched with start_new_session=True, so the `claude` process is a SESSION
LEADER of its own new session, and every guest it starts is a descendant inside THAT session —
never the runner's. The guard therefore cannot fire on a real guest.

MEASURED, not just argued: raw/11's REAL arm launched its guest through exactly that shape
(subprocess.Popen(..., start_new_session=True) wrapping systemd-run --user --scope), and the
guest WAS reaped — "SIGTERM ignored for 5s, SIGKILLed". If the session guard reached real
guests, that reap could not have happened.

=== VERDICT: the guard is a floor under the runner, not a ceiling on the reaper. ===
