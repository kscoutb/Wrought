Three held/expired pairs arrived, against the three SendMessage calls this session made (one of

No attempt is made here to map notices to individual sends one-to-one — the child PIDs are gone

received says HELD FOR APPROVAL, then NOT APPROVED BEFORE EXPIRY, then NOT DELIVERED.

=== WHAT THIS CORRECTS ===

raw/16 concluded: "on THIS build, a `-p` child does not appear to drain an inbox, so messages
sent to it were not delivered." That inference was reasonable from what was visible at the time
and IT IS WRONG ABOUT THE MECHANISM. The messages were not ignored by the child. They never
reached the child at all, because the delivery path interposes an explicit APPROVAL GATE held by
the RECIPIENT'S USER, and a headless `claude -p` gate child has no human sitting at it to approve
anything. The messages were held, went unapproved, and expired.

A second, smaller correction: raw/16 and REPORT-RUNNER S7.1 both say `SendMessage` "reports
delivery success". IT DOES NOT. It reported that the SEND WAS ACCEPTED — `{"success":true,
"msg_id":...}` — which is not the same claim, and the distinction is exactly the one this whole
gate keeps re-learning: an interface's optimistic acknowledgement is not evidence that the thing
happened. The delivery notices are the evidence, and they arrived minutes later.

=== WHAT IS NOW ESTABLISHED, AND WHAT STILL IS NOT ===

ESTABLISHED, and it is a STRONGER safety property than raw/16 credited: cross-session delivery to
a gate child is gated on a human approval that a headless child cannot give. In three attempts,
zero messages were delivered. The failure mode is fail-closed BY CONSTRUCTION of the delivery
path, not by luck and not by the child ignoring its inbox.

STILL NOT ESTABLISHED, and the GATE-RUNNER Phase-3 requirement is STILL NOT SATISFIED:
  - The approval gate is a PLATFORM behaviour, not something this gate configured. The prompt
    asked to "config the box so the runner's `claude` invocations can't be steered". No such
    configuration was found or applied. What protects the children is someone else's design
    decision, which is a fine thing to benefit from and a bad thing to depend on silently.
  - It is unpinned and unversioned in this project's terms. It could change in any CLI update,
    with no signal, exactly like every other version-dependent behaviour this gate measured.
    `claude_code_version = 2.1.238` is pinned in PROPOSED-PINS-DELTA for precisely this reason.
  - A gate child REMAINS DISCOVERABLE AND ADDRESSABLE. Nothing here changes raw/16's finding that
    neither env scrubbing nor a private XDG_RUNTIME_DIR removes it from ListAgents.
  - WHAT HAPPENS IF A HUMAN APPROVES was never tested, and is the case that actually matters: the
    operator is the one person on this box who could approve such a message, and the runner is
    designed to run while the operator is away. Whether an approved message would then reach and
    steer a `-p` child IS UNKNOWN.

=== DISPOSITION ===

The residual risk is unchanged in kind and smaller in degree than raw/16 stated. It is recorded,
not closed. The honest one-line version for the operator: the gate children cannot be steered by
a peer session without a human approving it, that approval gate is not ours and is not pinned by
us, and nobody has tested what an approved message does.
